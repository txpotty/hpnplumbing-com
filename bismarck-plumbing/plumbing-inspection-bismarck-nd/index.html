    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Bismarck

    Standard plumbing inspections usually cost $150 to $300. Adding a sewer camera inspection usually brings the total to $250 to $500.

    High water bills, repeat clogs, slow drains and discolored water are common signs. Noisy pipes are another. Strange sounds from the pipes or the water heater, along with visible moisture or mold near fixtures, usually point to an underlying problem that needs a professional look before it gets worse.

    You can do a basic check by looking under sinks and watching your water meter. The tools make the difference. A professional inspection adds sewer camera video, pressure readings, leak tracing and code knowledge, which together catch problems that a visual check simply cannot see.

    Every one to two years works for most homes. Some houses need it yearly. If your home has original supply pipe, large trees near the sewer or a history of leaks, a yearly inspection catches problems while they are still small and cheap to fix.

    Looking for drips and watching the meter is worth doing on your own. It just goes only so far. A professional inspection uses cameras, pressure gauges and leak detection tools to check the parts you cannot see, including the sewer line and the pipe inside walls, which is where the expensive problems usually hide.

    Most residential inspections in Bismarck take one to two hours. Age and size matter. The exact time depends on how big the house is, how old the plumbing is and whether we find something that needs extra tracing, such as a hidden leak or a sewer defect.

    Our technicians inspect all major water heater brands, including Rheem, AO Smith and Bradford White. Tankless units are covered. We also check Rinnai and Noritz tankless heaters and the common fixture brands found in most homes, so the brand on your equipment will not limit what we can inspect.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Gurgling drains count too. A damp wall, a musty cabinet or a warm spot on the floor can mean a hidden leak, and an inspection finds it before it spreads.
