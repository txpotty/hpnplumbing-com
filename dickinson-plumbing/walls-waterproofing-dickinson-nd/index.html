    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Dickinson, ND

    Foundation Crack Repair in Dickinson

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin starting at the lowest port and working up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Dickinson

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Dickinson, ND

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized for the inflow, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. Without one, the discharge water runs back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed at least 10 feet from the house.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Dickinson

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, lapped 12 inches at the seams and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. It is a cheap fix that makes a big difference.

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation.
