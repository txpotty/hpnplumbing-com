    Sump Pit Installation in Dickinson, ND

    Pit size controls how often the pump starts. Too small a pit means the pump short cycles in heavy flow, so we usually install an 18 by 22 inch or 18 by 30 inch basin with a solid bottom and perforated sides wrapped in filter fabric. Less cycling, longer life.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Dickinson

    No pit yet? First we locate the low point where water gathers, saw cut the slab, dig roughly 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a liner surrounded by washed gravel so water can enter while fine soil stays out. New concrete goes back flush and level.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Dickinson

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the water. The motor stays dry and you can reach it without pulling the pump, which is why they suit narrow pits under 14 inches across, and why they often outlast cheap submersibles in Dickinson homes. Expect more noise.

    Wayne Sump Pump Installation in Dickinson, ND

    Wayne builds everything from basic utility pumps to combo units with a built-in backup.     Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Dickinson, ND

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. Spring snowmelt is the main flooding risk in North Dakota, raising rivers and water tables and pushing water into basements. We add a second pump in the same pit with its own float, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. Your primary pump still handles the everyday load.

    Residential Basement Sump Pump Installation in Dickinson

    A typical house pit holds an 18 by 22 inch basin with a 1/3 horsepower pump, and that covers most homes. We size the pump to your inflow and lift, not to the sale sticker. In Dickinson, we set the basin at the low corner, bed it in washed gravel and run 1-1/2 inch PVC out through the rim joist. The discharge ends well away from the foundation.
