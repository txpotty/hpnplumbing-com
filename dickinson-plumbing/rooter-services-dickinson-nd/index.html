    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Dickinson

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. After-hours calls cost more.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. A jetter uses water at thousands of psi to scour the inside of the pipe from wall to wall. Jetting leaves the pipe cleaner. Snaking handles one-time clogs, while jetting is for grease lines and scaled mains that keep coming back.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

    Most single-drain clogs take 1 to 3 hours from arrival to flow test. A root-packed main or a job that adds jetting can take most of a day. Access makes the biggest difference. A buried or missing cleanout means pulling a toilet first, which adds about an hour.

    Not when the right cable and cutter are used at the right speed. Pipe that is already cracked, crushed or badly corroded can be harmed by any cleaning method. A camera check prevents surprises. On an older line with a history of trouble, we often look first so we know what we are cutting through.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They are dangerous to handle. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Dickinson's older homes take the most damage. They also put our techs at risk. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. Cables can kink, whip and wrap around a wrist in a second. Rentals jam in pipes. Getting a rental cable stuck in clay pipe can mean digging it out, which costs far more than a rooter visit.
