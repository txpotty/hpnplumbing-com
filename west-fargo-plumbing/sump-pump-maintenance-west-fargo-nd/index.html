Where groundwater is hard, mineral deposits can build up on the pump's impeller and in the pit, reducing efficiency and potentially causing clogs or damage over time. Regular maintenance helps limit these effects.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, hums without moving water, cycles very often or makes grinding noises. Rust, a stuck float or a musty smell around the pit are also red flag signs.

    Watch out for odd noises, such as grinding or rattling, that point to debris or a failing motor. If the pump runs constantly, fails to turn on or gives off a foul odor from the pit, it's clearly time for a professional check. Visible rust or corrosion are also red flags.

    With proper maintenance, a quality sump pump can last 7 to 10 years. Neglecting maintenance can greatly shorten its lifespan, leading to premature failure and costly replacements.
