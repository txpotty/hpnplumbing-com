    Thawing alone usually runs a couple hundred dollars. After-hours calls cost more. Reach, thawing time and any repair afterward set the price, and we quote the repair first.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. The line is still too cold. We will look at why it froze again and quote insulation, heat cable or a reroute into a heated space, because patching the same exposed run every winter costs more than fixing the cause once.

    Pipe location matters more than brand. Metal pipe cracks more easily. Older galvanized steel and copper in uninsulated attics, crawlspaces and outside walls are the most likely to split, while PEX in the same spot may freeze but often survives, though its fittings can still fail.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Bigger work might. Repiping, rerouting or replacing a backflow device may need one, which we handle.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. Water drains out of the stem after shutoff. Leaving a hose attached defeats it, since trapped water can split the valve inside the wall.

    Shut off the main water valve right away. Then turn off the water heater. Move belongings, drain the lines from a low faucet, and call us for the repair.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Pipes inside walls take more time. Lines in walls or plastic pipe can take a few hours with gentler heat.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Open flame is never safe here.
