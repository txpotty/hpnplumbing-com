    Gravity Grease Interceptor Installation in Fargo, ND

    Concrete or polyethylene, a buried gravity interceptor is a two-compartment tank with tees on the inlet and outlet that reach down below the grease layer. Precast concrete weighs more and handles a parking lot better than plastic. Poly is easier to set. With a plastic tank in wet ground we add anti-flotation anchoring, because an empty tank in saturated soil can pop right out of the hole.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Fargo

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. We also set the unit level within a quarter inch, since a tilted trap short-circuits the baffles and loses capacity.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Setup in Fargo, ND

    Small cafes and coffee bars often get by with a 10 to 25 GPM hydromechanical trap tucked under the prep sink or beside it. The unit goes on the floor or in a shallow recess, and the flow control fitting goes upstream. Clearance matters most. Your staff needs to lift the lid and clean it weekly, so we leave space to do that without unbolting anything.

    Automatic Grease Removal Units in Fargo

    An automatic grease removal unit heats the trapped grease and skims it into a container on a timer, so staff empty a bucket instead of scraping a tank. They cost more up front. They also need a dedicated electrical circuit, which we coordinate with your electrician before the unit arrives. Fry-heavy kitchens like them most.

    Restaurant Grease Trap Compliance in Fargo, ND

    Most sewer authorities run a FOG program with an inspector who opens your trap and checks your pump-out manifests against the schedule they set. A failed inspection can bring a fine or an order to upgrade. We install to pass. We add a sampling port if they require it and size to the authority's own formula.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Fargo, ND

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer.     Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Fargo

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. For fry-heavy kitchens we also set automatic units that skim grease on a timer. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    On a commercial kitchen, no. The work needs a licensed plumber and a permit in nearly every jurisdiction. For a home kitchen you might be allowed to, as long as the flow control and venting are done correctly. Ask the building department first.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. We file both when needed and give you copies of the approvals, which your health inspector may ask to see at opening. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    Two warranties apply here. Manufacturer coverage on the unit varies by brand and material, and plastic bodies usually get the longer terms. We cover our workmanship, meaning leaks at our joints and settling of a tank we set, and we give you the terms in writing before we start.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow floor sinks, grease odor at the trap, a lid that no longer seals, or a failed FOG inspection all point the same way, and we can tell you which it is in one visit.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. Strictly, a grease trap is the small indoor unit rated in gallons per minute, and an interceptor is the large tank rated in gallons of capacity. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.
