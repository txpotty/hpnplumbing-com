    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Maintenance in Fargo

    The cost for tankless water heater maintenance in Fargo, ND typically ranges from $150 to $300, depending on the unit's age, condition and the extent of descaling required. This includes a full flush and inspection of critical components. More complex issues or additional repairs would be quoted on their own.

    HPN Plumbing services all major brands of tankless water heaters, including Rinnai, Noritz, Navien, Bosch, State, Rheem and more. Our technicians are trained on the specific maintenance requirements and diagnostics for a broad range of models to provide expert care for your unit.

    It is recommended to have your tankless water heater professionally serviced at least once a year. In areas with very hard water, or for heavy usage, more frequent maintenance, such as every six to nine months, might be worth it to prevent scale buildup and maintain efficiency.

    With regular descaling, many tankless heaters last 15 to 20 years. Neglected units in hard water can fail in less than half that time.

    A softener or scale prevention system sharply reduces buildup in the heat exchanger. Some brands even recommend treatment when hardness is high. Many rural homes get water from private wells or rural water systems, and iron and sulfur odors are common complaints.

    Common signs include reduced hot water flow, fluctuating water temperatures, unusual noises coming from the unit, or error codes displayed on the control panel. If you notice any of these, it's a strong sign that your tankless water heater in Fargo is due for professional maintenance.

    A standard tankless water heater maintenance service, including flushing and inspection, usually takes 1 to 2 hours. The exact time can vary depending on the unit's condition, the amount of scale buildup and any unforeseen issues encountered during the service.

    The descaling process involves shutting off the unit, isolating the water lines and connecting a circulating pump to flush a mild acidic solution through the heat exchanger. This solution dissolves mineral deposits like calcium and magnesium, which are common in hard water, improving heat transfer efficiency.
