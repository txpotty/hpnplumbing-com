    Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in Devils Lake

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules allow. We size them to avoid too much pressure loss on your system.

    Pressure Vacuum Breaker (PVB) Installation in Devils Lake, ND

    For low-hazard applications, such as lawn irrigation systems in Devils Lake, ND, a pressure vacuum breaker, or PVB, is a common and cost-effective backflow prevention device. PVBs protect against back-siphonage by letting air into the system when pressure drops. Our certified plumbers expertly install PVBs above the highest point of downstream piping, as code requires, to maintain effective protection. We also check for proper clearance and drainage.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Devils Lake

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Devils Lake, ND typically ranges from $400 to $1,500, depending on the type of device, its size and the complexity of the installation. A basic residential PVB for an irrigation system is generally on the lower end, while a commercial-grade RPZ assembly costs more because of material and labor requirements.

    You probably need a backflow preventer if you have an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler system, a swimming pool or any auxiliary water source that connects to the main water supply. Local regulations in Devils Lake often mandate these devices for certain applications to protect public health.

    Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and boilers.

    It depends on the hazard and local rules. Irrigation often uses a PVB or double check, while high hazard connections like commercial kitchens or chemical injection require an RPZ.

    Most residential installs take two to four hours including testing. Large commercial assemblies can take a full day, especially if piping must be reconfigured.

    In Devils Lake, most backflow preventers must be tested annually by a certified backflow tester to confirm they are working correctly and providing adequate protection. This is a separate service from installation but is crucial for ongoing compliance and safety.

    Constant dripping or discharge from an RPZ relief valve, low pressure downstream and a failed annual test are typical signs. Visible corrosion or a cracked body from freezing also means service is needed.

    Most municipalities require annual testing of backflow preventers by a licensed, certified backflow tester. This verifies the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination. We can also help with scheduling these annual tests.
