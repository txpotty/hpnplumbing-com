    Standard Gas Water Heater Replacement in Devils Lake

    A standard 40 or 50 gallon atmospheric tank is what most homes have, and swapping one like for like is a two to three hour job when the venting and gas line are in good shape. We drain the old tank, cap the gas, and set the new heater on a stand or pan. New flex connectors every time. We soap-test the gas joints and check the draft hood with the burner lit.

    Propane Gas Water Heater Replacement in Devils Lake, ND

    Propane heaters run at about 11 inches of water column instead of the 7 inches typical for natural gas, so the gas valve and orifice have to match the fuel. We never swap a natural gas tank onto propane without the right conversion. We confirm pressure with a manometer.

    Rheem Gas Water Heater Service in Devils Lake, ND

    Rheem tanks and their Ruud twins share most parts, which helps when a gas valve fails a few years in. We install both. Setup follows the factory manual, including the draft test.

    A.O. Smith Gas Water Heater Service in Devils Lake

    A.O. Smith makes tanks under its own name and also builds the State line, so parts overlap a lot between the two brands. We handle the sizing and the gas connection. Exhaust venting gets checked against their specs.

    Bradford White Gas Water Heater Installation in Devils Lake

    Bradford White tanks are sold only through plumbing wholesalers, not big-box stores, and plenty of North Dakota plumbers install them for that reason. Parts are easy to get. We follow their venting and clearance specs to the letter, which keeps the warranty valid.

    Large Capacity 75 Gallon Gas Heaters in Devils Lake, ND

    Big families with soaking tubs often outgrow a 50 gallon tank and need a 75 gallon heater or a high-recovery 50 at around 65,000 to 75,000 BTU. Those burners need more gas. We check the meter and pipe size first, because a starved burner never reaches its rated recovery.

    High-Efficiency Gas Water Heater Upgrades in Devils Lake, ND

    Condensing gas tanks run around 90 percent efficient or better and vent through PVC, while a standard atmospheric tank sits closer to 60 percent. You will need a condensate drain and a nearby outlet. Big households see payback sooner.
