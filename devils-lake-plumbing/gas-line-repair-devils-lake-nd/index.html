    Gas repairs that change piping and all new gas lines usually need a permit and inspection. Our office files it and books the inspection.

    Utilities lock the meter when they find a leak or a failed test. A licensed plumber has to repair it and pass a pressure test before service is restored.

    We work on black iron, coated steel, CSST such as Gastite and TracPipe, and polyethylene for buried lines. Each material has its place. Polyethylene can only run underground and needs a listed riser where it comes up, while CSST needs bonding and black iron needs proper support and gas-rated thread sealant.

    We repair outdoor lines for grills, fire pits, pool heaters and patio heaters. Outdoor lines take more abuse. Sun, sprinklers and lawn equipment all wear on exposed piping, so we use coated steel or properly buried polyethylene and check that the line is sized for the appliances on it.

    Simple repairs take an hour or two. Yard line replacements usually take a day, and permitted work adds inspection time.

    A rotten egg smell is the big one, since utilities add a sulfur odorant to natural gas. Listen for hissing.
