    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Horace

    A minor, accessible pinhole repair starts around $200. Slab leaks run higher. Complex repairs like under-slab leaks can run past $2,000, depending on where the leak is, how bad it is and how much copper needs replacing. We price it after we find it.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. They will not last. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, and a DIY patch that lets go while you are at work can flood a room in the time it takes to drive home. Use it only until a plumber arrives.

    The pipe wall wears through from the inside. Water chemistry drives it. Aggressive water, high velocity, excess pressure, poor original soldering and stray electrical current from grounding issues each thin the wall until it pins through. Hot water speeds it up.

    Shut off the main water valve. Then call (701) 403-1348. Turning off the main stops the damage while you wait, and opening a low faucet drains the pressure out of the line so the leak slows to a drip. Get anything important out of the way. We will walk you through it if needed.

    Keep pressure in range. Test it yearly. Soften hard water, limit recirculation pump run time and get any green crust on fittings looked at early, while homes with older copper should have it checked every few years. Early repairs cost less.

    A repair clamp or epoxy can buy time in an emergency. It is not permanent. A plumber should cut out the damaged section and install proper fittings, particularly when the leak is somewhere you cannot see. Shut the water off first.

    An accessible pinhole or a single joint takes one to four hours. Slab leaks take longer. Leaks under a slab or a repipe can take a full day or more, depending on access, whether the line is rerouted and how much copper needs replacing. We give you a timeline with the price.
