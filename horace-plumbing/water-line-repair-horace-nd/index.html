    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Horace

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older homes may still have galvanized supply lines or cast iron drains, which can corrode and restrict flow with age. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Horace

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. We confirm it in about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and continued movement tells us the leak is in the buried line. Ground microphones find the spot. We cut in a repair coupling or new pipe and pressure test before backfill.

    Water Pressure Restoration in Horace

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. We put a gauge on a hose bib first. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the house shutoff and the meter-side valve for a partly closed gate, and look at the pipe itself. Old galvanized can be choked to half its size with rust. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Horace, ND

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. Once one spot leaks, the pipe on either side is usually just as thin. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, and sometimes that buys a few good years. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. You get your flow back as a bonus.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Horace

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. Trenchless avoids most of that. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, following the old line or cutting a new path beside it. Most replacements finish in a day. You also get the manufacturer warranty on the new pipe.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Horace

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, while new HDPE comes through right behind the head. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Horace, ND

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Main Water Line Repair in Horace

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. A leak here shows up as a soggy strip of lawn and a meter dial that never rests. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.
