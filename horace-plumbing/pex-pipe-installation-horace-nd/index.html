    Copper to PEX Conversions in Horace

    Pinhole leaks in copper tend to come in clusters. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Many rural homes get water from private wells or rural water systems, and iron and sulfur odors are common complaints. Converting to PEX stops the cycle. Where new PEX meets existing copper stubs or valves, we use listed transition fittings.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Horace

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. We stretch the pipe and ring, seat the fitting, and within seconds the plastic shrinks back around the barb into a tight seal. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Horace

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. Since a crimp is only as good as the tool that made it, every ring gets a go/no-go gauge check, and any ring that fails gets cut off and redone. That takes seconds. Skipping it is how leaks start.
