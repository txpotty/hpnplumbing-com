    PEX Repiping in Grand Forks, ND

    PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run long distances without a fitting, which means fewer joints hidden in your walls. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. PEX shrugs off scale and chlorine, survives freezing better than copper and installs faster, so it is our most common repipe material. We use PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter of the pipe open. That keeps pressure up at the far fixtures.

    Type L Copper Repiping in Grand Forks

    Copper has been plumbing homes for generations, and done right it lasts a long time. We use Type L, not the thinner Type M, and we support it with the right hangers so it does not rub or rattle in the walls. All joints use lead-free solder. Because some water chemistries eat copper, we look at your leak history before recommending it over PEX.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Grand Forks

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. We use Uponor or similar PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that open the pipe end and let it shrink back over the fitting for a full-bore joint. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Most homes are repiped with PEX-A in two to three days.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Grand Forks, ND

    Older homes may still have galvanized steel pipe that rusts from the inside out. Weak flow and rusty water are common. Old galvanized gets removed or abandoned and replaced with PEX or copper, and most homes see better pressure and clearer water right away.

    Repipe Costs Versus Leak Repairs in Grand Forks, ND

    A whole-home PEX repipe for an average house often runs $5,000 to $12,000, with copper costing more. A single slab leak repair can run $1,000 to $4,000 once access and restoration are included.     Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Grand Forks

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Many homes built from the 1980s through the early 2000s have one or the other. The pipe at the water heater usually tells us which one you have, and we replace the full system so the home is easier to insure and to sell.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Grand Forks, ND

    Water softeners and filters are popular around Grand Forks because they extend the life of water heaters, dishwashers and faucets. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. In hard water homes, PEX-A is often the better pick since it resists scale, and a softener loop or filter can be added during the job. Adding the loop now costs less than later.
