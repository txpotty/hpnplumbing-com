    Resin Bed Checks and Regeneration Settings in Grand Forks

    Resin does the actual softening. Each bead trades sodium for calcium and magnesium until it runs out of capacity. We compare the softened hardness against the gallons used since the last regeneration to tell whether the bed is still at full strength.

    Resin Cleaning and Regeneration Tuning in Grand Forks, ND

    Iron is the most common resin fouler we see. The beads turn rusty brown and stop grabbing hardness. We feed a resin cleaner through the brine well, run a regeneration and retest, and if the capacity comes back we shorten the time between regenerations so the iron does not build back up.

    Resin Rebedding in Grand Forks

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. A resin cleaner is the first thing we try. If the bed is spent, we drain the tank, pull the old resin and refill it with fresh high-capacity resin, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Softener Maintenance Plans and Pricing in Grand Forks, ND

    The plan is one flat yearly fee. It covers the service visit and both hardness tests, and parts installed on a plan visit come at a reduced price. For most homes it pays for itself the first time it catches a stuck float.

    Commercial Softener Service in Grand Forks

    Commercial units work harder. Restaurants and laundromats can burn through a softener's capacity in a single day, so a twin-tank system keeps one tank online while the other recharges. We set visits around your business hours so the water never goes hard during a rush.

    Control Valve and Timer Diagnostics in Grand Forks, ND

    The control valve runs every stage of regeneration, from backwash through brine draw to the final refill. We step it through each cycle by hand and watch for a motor that stalls, a drive gear that skips, a piston that drags or a drain that never shuts off. Worn pistons turn up a lot.

    Brine Tank Cleanout and Inspection in Grand Forks, ND

    Brine tanks collect a gray sludge of salt fines and insoluble grit, and it builds up faster with cheap rock salt. We empty, scrub and rinse the tank. Then we look for cracks near the overflow elbow and the brine well and refill with fresh salt.
