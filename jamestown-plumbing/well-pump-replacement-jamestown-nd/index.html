    Submersible and Jet Pump Work in Jamestown, ND

    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Jamestown

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. On the way up, we look at the torque arrestor and the splices. Most jobs are done in a day.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Jamestown, ND

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. Air sneaks in through a pinhole in the suction line, the pump loses prime and the seal burns up running dry. We pressure test the line. A well that is too deep for suction needs a submersible, and we will quote that instead.

    Well Pump Brands We Install in Jamestown, ND

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Sta-Rite cover nearly every well pump we install. Each brand has strengths, and we choose based on your well. Goulds and Franklin parts are on every supply shelf, which helps when a control box fails late on a Saturday afternoon.

    Constant Pressure Well Pump Installation in Jamestown, ND

    Constant pressure systems hold the house at one pressure, say 50 psi, whether one faucet is open or the shower and the irrigation are running together. The VFD changes the motor speed to match how much water you are using. No more pressure dip mid-shower.

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Jamestown

    Sand kills submersibles early. A well that pumps sand will wear impellers down in a few years, so we check for grit in the old pump and talk about setting the new one higher off the bottom or adding a sand separator. Where the pump hangs matters.

    Drop Pipe and Wire Replacement in Jamestown

    Galvanized drop pipe from the 1970s and 1980s rusts from the inside, and a pinhole at 150 feet will bleed pressure every time the pump shuts off. Depending on depth and pump weight, it gets replaced with schedule 120 PVC or poly. Wire gets the same look. Cracked insulation and taped splices get cut out.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Jamestown

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. We megger the motor before we pull it. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Jamestown

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. Both use a small 2 to 20 gallon tank instead of a big one, since the drive keeps the pump running at a matched speed. Soft starts also cut wear on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.
