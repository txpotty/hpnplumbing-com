    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Maintenance in Wahpeton

    A standard inspection and cleaning typically runs $125 to $250. Replacing a float switch or check valve typically adds $75 to $250 for parts and labor.

    Watch for a pump that runs nonstop, hums without moving water, cycles every minute or two, or grinds. A stuck float, rust on the housing or a musty smell near the pit also point to trouble.

    Listen for grinding or rattling during a cycle, which usually means debris caught in the impeller or a bearing that is starting to fail. Constant running, a pump that will not kick on or a foul smell from the pit all call for a visit. Rust on the pump body is another red flag.

    Our crew services Zoeller, Liberty, Wayne, Little Giant, Superior and most other residential and commercial pumps. We can also size an upgrade for a pit that cannot keep up.

    Routine maintenance in Wahpeton, ND usually does not need a permit. Replacing the whole unit, rerouting the discharge line or adding a new electrical circuit sometimes does, depending on the local building department. You will know before we begin if a permit applies.

    Seven to ten years is normal for a quality pump. A pump left dry for months can seize early, so testing during dry spells matters.

    Most local codes prohibit sump discharge into the sanitary sewer, since storm water can overload treatment plants. We route it to the yard, a dry well or another approved spot.
