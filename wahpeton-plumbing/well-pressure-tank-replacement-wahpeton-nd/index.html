    Well-Rite Pressure Tank Installation in Wahpeton, ND

    Well-Rite tanks use a replaceable diaphragm design in many models. We install them in the common residential sizes. Like any tank, the key is setting pre-charge before it fills, and checking that drawdown matches the pump. A good tank installed badly fails early.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Wahpeton, ND

    Switch contacts pit and burn over time. A pitted switch can stick on or chatter. When we swap a tank we look at the contacts and the sensing port underneath, since a new tank paired with a tired switch is asking for trouble. Cheap part.

    Well Pressure Tank Leak Repair vs. Replacement in Wahpeton

    Leaks at the tank tee, a gauge or a fitting can be fixed. A leak from the tank itself cannot. Bladder tanks with a fixed bladder cannot be rebuilt, so a torn bladder or rusted seam means a new tank, while a leaking tee or relief valve is a simple part swap we can usually do in under an hour.

    Galvanized Steel Pressure Tank Upgrades in Wahpeton

    Old galvanized tanks have no bladder at all. Air and water share the same space. Over time the water absorbs the air, the tank waterlogs, and someone has to drain and recharge it every few months. A sealed bladder tank ends that chore.

    Pressure Tanks for Well Houses and Barns in Wahpeton, ND

    Tanks in outbuildings face heat, cold and damp. Steel rusts fast in a damp well house. We often recommend a composite tank in those spaces, raise it off the floor on a stand, and insulate exposed piping and the switch when the building sees freezing weather. Simple protection.

    Well-X-Trol Pressure Tank Installation in Wahpeton, ND

    Amtrol's Well-X-Trol line uses a diaphragm design. We put in plenty of them. Its stainless or polypropylene water connection keeps water off the steel shell, limiting internal corrosion, and the factory pre-charge still gets checked and adjusted to your switch on install.

    Flex-Lite Pressure Tank Replacement in Wahpeton

    Flex-Lite tanks have a composite shell. They will not rust. That makes them a good pick for damp basements, crawlspaces and well houses where steel tanks tend to corrode from the bottom up, plus they are easier to move into cramped utility rooms. Same plumbing as steel.
