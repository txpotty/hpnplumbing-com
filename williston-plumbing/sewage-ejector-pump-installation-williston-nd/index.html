    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Williston

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin usually costs $900 to $2,000. A new ejector system with basin, venting and floor work typically ranges from $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a high-quality sewage ejector pump can last 7 to 15 years. Factors such as usage frequency, the type of waste it handles and water quality can influence its lifespan. Annual inspections can help extend its longevity.

    A sump pump removes groundwater or seepage, while an ejector pump handles sewage and wastewater from fixtures. Ejector pumps are designed to pass solids and require a sealed, vented basin.

    Pump replacements typically take two to four hours. New systems with floor excavation and venting typically take one to two days.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional inspect your system to prevent a complete failure and costly backups.

    We install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne ejector pumps, plus Saniflo macerating systems. We choose the brand and model according to your fixtures and lift height.

    We recommend having your sewage ejector pump inspected annually, especially if it is in a high-use area. Regular inspections can identify potential issues early, extend the life of your pump and prevent unexpected failures and costly backups. This is particularly important for older units.

    Regular maintenance for a sewage ejector pump involves periodically checking the basin for debris, confirming the check valve works correctly and testing the float switch. It is also wise to avoid flushing non-biodegradable items. HPN Plumbing offers maintenance services to keep your pump running efficiently and prevent unexpected breakdowns.
