    Disposal Installation for Double Sinks in Williston, ND

    On a double sink, the disposal goes on one bowl and a continuous waste or end outlet tee on the other. We lay out the drain so both bowls drain freely and the disposal outlet does not push water into the other bowl, which happens when the tee sits too close to the disposal discharge. We use heavy-wall fittings.

    Commercial Disposal Installation in Williston

    Commercial disposals run 1 to 5 horsepower and usually need a dedicated circuit, a control panel and a water solenoid. We set the unit on the mounting collar, run the drain to the grease trap where the local rules require it, and wire the controls so the water runs whenever the motor does. Health codes vary. We check the requirement before we order anything.

    Disposal Installation on an Existing Sink in Williston

    For a common 1/2 or 3/4 HP disposal, we remove the old unit if there is one and install the new one on the existing sink. We connect it to the drain, set the P-trap and wire it, then check the clearance under the sink, which can be tight in older Williston homes with shallow cabinets or a water filter mounted on the back wall. Cramped cabinets take some planning. A compact unit can solve it.

    Switching From Batch-Feed to Continuous-Feed in Williston, ND

    Going from batch-feed to continuous-feed means adding a way to switch it on. That can be a wall switch, an air switch button on the sink deck or a switched outlet under the sink, and the choice depends on how hard it is to run wire to the spot you want. Air switches avoid new wiring in the wall. The drain usually stays the same.

    Batch-Feed Disposal Installation in Williston, ND

    Batch-feed units only run with the lid in place, and the lid holds a magnet that closes a switch inside the unit. No wall switch is needed, which makes them handy in kitchens where running a new switch would mean opening finished walls. You grind one load at a time. Families with small kids often like the trade.

    Standard Disposal Installation in Williston

    Most households in Williston do well with a 1/3 to 1/2 horsepower continuous-feed unit. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Then we wire it and test it. A smooth spin and a clean drain tell us it is right.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Williston

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit is bigger and heavier than a starter model, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. Power needs a check too. A larger motor pulls more current when it starts.
