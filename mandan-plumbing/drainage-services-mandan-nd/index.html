    Most homes never need scheduled drain cleaning. A slow drain is the signal. Root-prone sewers and grease-heavy kitchens benefit from cleaning every one to two years.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It works fast and costs less. Jetting scours the full pipe wall, lasting longer, but it costs more and needs sound pipe.

    Roots usually enter through an existing crack or joint, then widen it as they grow. Over years, that can split clay pipe. Roots can offset joints and, in bad cases, collapse the pipe, which then needs digging or lining.

    We advise against it. Caustic cleaners can soften old PVC joints and eat at cast iron and metal traps. They also rarely clear a full blockage, and when they fail, the plumber has to work around a trap full of caustic liquid that can burn skin and eyes, which slows the job and raises the risk.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It is fast and inexpensive. Jetting is better for grease-coated kitchen lines, scaled cast iron and roots that keep coming back, because it cleans the pipe wall instead of punching a hole through the blockage.

    Clearing a clog does not need a permit. Repairs to the pipe itself often do. Replacing a section of sewer, adding a cleanout, or connecting a new drain to the sewer generally requires a permit and an inspection, and we handle that paperwork and schedule the inspector as part of the job.
