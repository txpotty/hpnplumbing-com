    Service Line Replacement Choices in Mandan, ND

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Mandan, ND

    Bursting fits old cast iron or galvanized lines that will shatter under the head. Pulling suits softer pipe like poly or copper. Either way the new pipe follows the old path, which matters when the route runs under a patio or a neighbor's fence, leaving you with two small holes instead of a long trench. Rock can stop it.

    PEX Service Lines for Long Runs in Mandan

    PEX comes in coils up to several hundred feet. That makes it the easy pick when the meter sits out at the road and the house is set far back, since a single coil reaches all the way without a splice. Fewer joints underground means fewer places a leak can start, and it is the joints that usually fail first.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Mandan, ND

    Pipe pulling works like this. Two pits get dug, one at each end. A cable or bursting head goes through the old pipe and drags new HDPE or PEX back through the same path, breaking up the old line if it has gone brittle, so a driveway, a row of shrubs or a concrete walk can stay where it is.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Mandan

    Your service line runs from the meter or curb stop to the main shutoff inside the house, usually as 3/4-inch or 1-inch pipe. Once it has leaked in two different places, patching stops making sense. We dig at both ends, pull or cut out the old pipe, and run new line at the depth local code calls for, with fresh connections at the meter and at the shutoff. Most homes are done in a day.

    Copper Service Line Installation in Mandan, ND

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. Plenty of copper mains have lasted 60 years. We flare or use brass compression fittings at the ends, keep the run in one piece between them, and pack sand around it so rocks do not touch the pipe. Where it goes through a concrete wall, it gets a plastic sleeve and a seal to keep water and bugs out.

    Commercial Water Line Replacement in Mandan

    Larger buildings bring larger pipe, deeper trenches and more inspection steps. We handle the backflow test and certification paperwork. Trench shoring comes into play below five feet, and we plan the work so the building is without water for hours, not days, often running a temporary supply while the new line is tied in. Night work is possible.

    Burst Main Line Response in Mandan, ND

    A burst main is a fast-moving problem. We get the water off, dig to the break, and decide on site whether a clamp or a short section of new pipe will hold until a full replacement can be scheduled, or whether the pipe crumbles when we touch it. You will not be without water overnight if we can help it.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Mandan

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? We will usually just fix that spot. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so replacing the whole run is cheaper over five years. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Replacement in Mandan

    For a typical house, figure on $2,500 to $8,000. Length and method move the number the most, because a trenchless pull under a driveway costs more per foot than an open trench across a lawn but spares you a concrete bill. Copper adds a bit. You get a written number.

    Repair fixes one spot. A short piece of new pipe goes in where the leak was. Replacement swaps the entire run from meter to house, which is the better choice once the old pipe is corroded everywhere or has failed more than once, since the next leak is usually a few feet away.

    One day for most homes. A long rural run, a trench through rock, or a job that needs an inspection before backfill can push it to two or three days, with the water back on each evening when we can manage it. Trenchless is often quicker than trenching because there is less digging to undo.

    Both commonly reach 50 years. Copper has the longer track record in the ground. PEX is newer but does not corrode, and the main things that kill it are UV light before burial and sharp rock, which a good crew prevents. Bedding counts.

    Strong winds and extreme cold can drive frost into walls and crawlspaces, reaching pipes that seem well protected. Homeowners around Mandan should insulate exposed pipes, drain outdoor spigots and keep the heat on when away in winter. Burying the line deep enough is what keeps it from freezing. We set new service lines at or below the frost depth local code requires, insulate the riser where it comes up to the meter, and use pipe that tolerates the swings, which in cold country often means PEX.

    Pressure that drops at every fixture at once is the big one. A wet or extra-green strip of lawn in a dry week is another. Add rusty water after a shutoff, a water bill that climbs without new usage, or a meter dial that spins with every tap closed, and we would test the main first. We test that first.

    Basements are standard in most homes, and many rely on sump pumps to manage groundwater during wet springs. Shifting ground pulls on fittings. Roots go after the moisture around a weeping joint, and sharp rock rubs through pipe that was buried without proper bedding, which is why we pad every new line with clean fill. That bedding matters more than the pipe brand.

    In most places the utility owns the main in the street and the line up to the meter. You own the line from the meter to the house. Some cities draw the line at the curb stop instead, so we check with the local provider before we start. That decides who pays.
