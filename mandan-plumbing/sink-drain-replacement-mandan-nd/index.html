    Push-Button Drain Installation in Mandan, ND

    Push-button drains get rid of the lift rod. They open and close with a press on the cap, and we check that the sink has an overflow before choosing a slotted body, since a slotted drain in a bowl without an overflow leaks out the slots. Cleaning is simpler with no linkage. Hair still collects, so pull the cap now and then.

    Commercial Lavatory Drain Replacement in Mandan

    Public restroom drains take constant use.     Utility and Basement Sink Drain Replacement in Mandan

    Basement sinks often drain to a pump. Sinks below the sewer line drain through a pump, so we check its valve and discharge line during the job. Pump trouble is easy to miss. A gurgling pit is a clue.

    Leaking P-Trap Replacement in Mandan

    Traps leak at the joints first. The beveled washers inside the slip nuts flatten and harden over the years, and a trap that has been taken apart several times to clear clogs often has stripped plastic threads or a cracked nut. A new trap kit costs little. Labor is most of the bill.

    Utility Sink Drain Replacement in Mandan

    Laundry tubs see lint and paint water. Lint and grit wear out thin utility tub drains and pack the trap so it never drains right. A 1-1/2 inch trap with a cleanout plug goes in. A lint trap on the washer hose helps.

    Bathroom Drain Upgrade in Mandan, ND

    Old bath drains often carry decades of hair and scum. Replacing the pop-up body and the trap removes the rough inside walls that grab hair, and a smooth new 1-1/4 inch path drains a full bowl in seconds instead of minutes. Clogs come back slower. A hair catcher keeps it that way.

    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Mandan

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Most glass and stone vessel bowls have no overflow hole, so they need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, and a slotted drain in one of those bowls leaks out of the slots right into the counter hole. We see the wrong drain often. Swapping it takes under an hour.
