    Well Pump Problems We Fix in Mandan, ND

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Service in Mandan, ND

    Franklin Electric motors and control boxes are in a lot of local wells. Our technicians know them well, from the QD control boxes to the SubDrive constant pressure units. Common Franklin parts are on the truck. A motor that tests bad on a megohm meter usually gets replaced rather than rewound, since a new motor costs less than shipping and rebuilding the old one.

    Submersible Pump Replacement in Mandan

    Sand wears impellers down over time, and a pump that once filled the tank in a minute starts taking three. The motor, the wire splice and the check valve are the other common failures. We test the motor and wire from the surface before deciding whether to pull. New pumps match your well's yield.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Mandan

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up, run quietly and last, but motors, impellers and splices still wear out. After pulling the pump, we test each part and replace only the failed one. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Mandan, ND well.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Mandan

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. HPN Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Mandan, ND well. Usually the electronics are fine.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in Mandan

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. In some cases the pump is fine and the well just cannot keep up with demand. We test each one.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Mandan, ND

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Heavy summer thunderstorms can also overwhelm storm sewers, so backwater valves help protect finished basements. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. We test capacitors, relays and overload protection, replace what failed and recommend a surge arrestor at the box if one is missing. Arrestors are cheap.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Mandan, ND

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. We check the air charge, inspect the bladder and recharge or replace the tank, then confirm the pump runs at least a minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in Mandan

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. These pumps can also leak at the shaft seal or wear out an impeller. We reprime the pump, replace the seal and check the foot valve and suction line for leaks, since one tiny air leak will undo the whole repair. Everything is above ground.
