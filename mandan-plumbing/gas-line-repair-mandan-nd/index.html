    Gas Leak Repair at Fittings and Valves in Mandan

    Most residential gas leaks are at a threaded joint, a valve stem or a flexible connector, not in the middle of a pipe. That keeps repairs small. We back out the leaking joint, clean the threads, apply gas-rated thread sealant and reassemble it, or replace the fitting or valve if the threads are damaged. We soap-test the repaired joint and pressure test the system before leaving.

    Gas Appliance Connector Repair in Mandan, ND

    Weak flames or a smell near one appliance often point to the connector or shutoff behind it. Older uncoated brass connectors can crack at the flare nut, and they are no longer accepted by most codes or utilities. We replace them with coated stainless connectors. We also add a reachable shutoff and a drip leg where needed, and confirm the new connector is rated for that appliance's BTU input.

    CSST Repair and Bonding in Mandan, ND

    Newer homes often have corrugated stainless steel tubing running through the attic. CSST can be damaged by nails, rodents or lightning, and older yellow-jacket CSST needs proper bonding to the electrical grounding system to reduce the risk of a lightning arc. Bonding is a short job. Damaged runs get spliced with the manufacturer's fittings, and we confirm the bond on the way out. Heavy summer thunderstorms can also overload storm sewers, so backwater valves help protect finished basements.

    Interior Black Iron Pipe Repair in Mandan

    Threaded black iron pipe can leak at joints after years of vibration or settling. Our plumbers reseal or replace the leaking fittings and sections using new pipe and approved sealant. Rusted pipe goes too. Heavily rusted pipe in garages, crawlspaces or outside walls is replaced before it fails, and we pressure test the full system afterward.
