    Ejector pumps tend to run 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed.     Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Valley City, ND

    The basin holds everything, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. A larger basin means longer, less frequent cycles that spare the motor and switch, so we go bigger when the pit takes a laundry or several fixtures. Every lid gets sealed.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Valley City, ND

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes often need duplex systems with an alternating control panel. The panel switches the lead pump each cycle so wear is shared, and if the lead pump fails or the flow is heavy, the second pump starts automatically and the panel trips an alarm so someone knows to call for service. Two pumps, one alarm.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in Valley City, ND

    Upgrading an old ejector does not always mean a new basin. If the basin is sound, we can swap in a better pump, replace the old swing check with a new one, add a full-port shutoff and install a high water alarm, which covers most of what a new system offers for a fraction of the cost. Cracked basins get replaced.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Valley City

    Residential ejector systems in Valley City serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer.     Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Valley City

    A high water alarm should be on any ejector basin, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. We install audible alarms with their own power source, or Wi-Fi alarms that text your phone, so you hear about a stuck float while the basin is still filling and not after waste has reached the basement floor. Heavy summer thunderstorms can also overwhelm storm sewers, so backwater valves help protect finished basements. Battery backup is an option.
