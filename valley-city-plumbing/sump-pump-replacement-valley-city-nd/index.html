    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Valley City

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle. We install new check valves with every pump replacement and verify the switch moves freely. Vertical float switches work well in tight pits. Tethered floats work best in larger pits.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Valley City

    A standard sump pump replacement typically costs $500 to $1,200 installed. Battery backup systems usually add $800 to $1,800. Sewage ejector pumps usually cost $1,200 to $2,500.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit. The pump should activate, drain the water and then shut off automatically. If it does not, it is time for a professional's inspection.

    While it might seem straightforward, professional sump pump replacement is strongly recommended. It involves electrical connections, proper plumbing for the discharge line, and making sure the pump is correctly sized and installed to prevent future flooding and comply with local Valley City codes.

    In many areas, minor like-for-like sump pump replacements typically do not require a permit. However, if the replacement involves major changes to the electrical system, discharge piping or the sump pit itself, a permit might be necessary. Our team will advise you on local code requirements in Valley City.

    Most standard sump pump replacements can be finished within 2 to 4 hours. More complex installations, such as those requiring new pit excavation or extensive discharge line work, might take longer, up to a full day.

    To maintain your new sump pump, regularly check the pit for debris, test the pump monthly by pouring water into the pit, and make sure the discharge pipe is clear. An annual professional inspection by HPN Plumbing can also help extend its lifespan.

    Constant running, loud grinding, frequent cycling or failure to start are common signs. Rust, age and a burning smell also indicate trouble.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a smart investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.
