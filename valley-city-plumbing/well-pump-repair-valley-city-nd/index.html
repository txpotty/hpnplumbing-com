    Well Pump Diagnostic Services in Valley City, ND

    Voltage, amperage, pressure and flow rate tell us most of what is happening down the well without pulling a single length of pipe. A weak motor draws high amps.     Pressure Tank Repair in Valley City, ND

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. The air charge gets checked and the bladder inspected, then the tank is recharged or replaced so the pump runs a full minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Pressure Tank Problems in Valley City

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. Usually the bladder has failed or the tank has lost its air charge. We check the precharge with the tank drained. It should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure, so a 40/60 switch wants roughly 38 psi in the tank.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in Valley City

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Valley City

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. Testing starts at the surface. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Sensor failures and drive errors are the usual problems. HPN Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Valley City, ND well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Valley City, ND

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Spring snowmelt is the main flooding risk in North Dakota, raising rivers and water tables and pushing water into basements. Surges from lightning are the usual cause of control box failures. We test capacitors, relays and overload protection, replace what failed and recommend a surge arrestor at the box if one is missing. An arrestor costs little.
