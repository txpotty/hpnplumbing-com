    Types of Toilet Installation in Minot AFB, ND

    Wall-Mounted Toilet Installation in Minot AFB

    Wall-mounted toilets hang off a steel carrier bolted to the floor inside the wall. The tank sits behind an access plate. Because the carrier carries all the weight, we anchor its feet to the slab or framing first and check it for plumb before the drywall or tile goes up. Plan on a remodel, not a swap.

    New Construction Toilet Installation in Minot AFB

    On new builds we set toilets after the finish floor is down, never before. A flange belongs flush with or just above finished flooring. An early set means the tile goes around the flange, leaving it low, and that calls for an extender ring later. Each bowl gets a fresh seal.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Installation in Minot AFB, ND

    Wall-hung toilets need a wall at least 2 by 6 deep, or a furred-out chase built in front of an existing 2 by 4 wall. Floors stay clear underneath. Cleaning gets easier, and the bowl height can be set anywhere within the carrier range, useful for a tall family. Service happens through the flush plate.

    Two-Piece Toilet Installation in Minot AFB

    Two-piece toilets come with the tank and bowl separate, joined by a rubber gasket and two or three bolts. The tank goes on before the bowl is set. Tightening the tank bolts evenly, side to side, matters more than how hard they go, because an uneven pull cracks the china or leaves a slow drip at one bolt. It should not wobble.

    Bathroom Remodel Toilet Installation in Minot AFB, ND

    New tile over old vinyl can raise a bathroom floor by half an inch or more. Suddenly the old flange is below the floor. We bring the flange up with a stacking extender or replace it outright, and if you are moving the toilet on a slab, that means cutting concrete to reroute the drain and patching it afterward. Moving it on a wood floor is easier.

    Urgent Toilet Replacement in Minot AFB

    A cracked tank or a bowl leaking at the base cannot wait for next week, especially in a house with one bathroom. Standard two-piece toilets ride on our trucks. In many cases we can pull the broken one and set a replacement in the same visit, with a 12 inch rough-in model in round or elongated. Close the stop until then.

    Smart Toilet Installation in Minot AFB, ND

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most require a GFCI outlet within reach of the cord. The water connection and mounting are ours, and when an outlet has to be added we schedule a licensed electrician instead of doing that wiring. Then we test every button.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Minot AFB, ND

    High-efficiency toilets flush on 1.28 gallons or less, against 3.5 gallons or more for toilets from before the early 1990s. Check for the WaterSense label. A MaP rating of 800 grams or better is what we look for, since a weak low-flow toilet that needs double flushing saves no water at all. Dual-flush models add a lighter half flush.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Minot AFB

    Labor for a standard toilet installation in Minot AFB usually runs $250 to $600, not counting the toilet itself. Flange repairs add to that. Extra work such as a rotted flange or soft subfloor raises the price, and we quote it before we begin.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common. Some older homes and tight bathrooms have 10 or 14 inch rough-ins, and a toilet built for the wrong distance either will not fit against the wall or leaves an awkward gap behind the tank. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    Lower water bills come first. An old 3.5 gallon toilet uses almost three times the water of a 1.28 gallon model. Modern bowls clog less thanks to wider glazed trapways, and the smoother porcelain is easier to keep clean.

    Both seal fine on a flange set at the right height. Wax has decades behind it. Foam or rubber wax-free seals forgive a toilet that has to be lifted and reset, and they do not soften the way wax can in a warm bathroom or over radiant floor heat. We pick based on your flange height.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Some jobs run a full day. Wall-hung carriers and slab relocations open walls or floors, so they get booked as separate projects with cure time built in.

    It depends on the floor and the flange. On a sound flange, a same-spot swap is fair game for a handy owner. New tile, a low or broken flange, a wall-hung carrier, or a toilet that has to move are the jobs where a plumber saves you money, because mistakes there end up under the floor.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Hairline cracks can be hard to spot. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    Plenty of handy homeowners do. What goes wrong most is cranked-down bolts or a bowl that rocks.
